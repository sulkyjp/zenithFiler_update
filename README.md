- ⚡ **Fast** — starts in about 1 second, every operation runs asynchronously. Optimized on real measurements to stay snappy even on antivirus-laden corporate PCs<br>**速い** — 起動約 1 秒・全操作を非同期化。ウイルス対策ソフトで重い社用 PC でも軽快に動くよう実測ベースで最適化

## Latest Changes — [1.16.4] - 2026-09-24 : The manual is split into per-feature articles so you reach what you want at once, a plain default theme with no theme color is added, and index syncing plus paste and delete problems are fixed

### Added

- **Added "Plain", a basic theme made only of white and light gray, and made it the default on first launch (#349)** — none of the themes so far was a plain, unadorned mix of white and light gray. The default "standard" is beige, and the other light themes either have a dark title bar or a tinted background, so there was nothing to pick when you would rather not be seen using something unusual. In "Plain" the file list is almost white, the sidebar and headers are light gray, and the title bar is a slightly darker gray so the layers stay distinct. **It has no theme color**: the address bar is a white field like File Explorer's, and selected tabs and buttons are shown in shades of gray. Blue appears only faintly, in the file list selection and the search box focus border. It is preselected in the theme choice on first launch and sits at the top of the Standard category in the theme list. If you already use Zenith Filer, your theme does not change.

### Changed

- **The manual is now split into one article per feature, so you can get straight to what you need (#345)** — the manual used to be one long document, and all of it (about 4,000 lines) was drawn every time it opened. It is now **60 articles** grouped into categories such as "Screen Layout", "Basic Operations" and "Search". Choosing a category and an article in the table of contents shows **just that article**, so opening and switching are both lighter, and the article's headings are listed beneath it. You can still **read straight through by scrolling**: at the end of an article a "Next article" bar appears, and **one more push** of the wheel from there moves on to the next article (one more push at the top goes back to the end of the previous one). You can keep scrolling without stopping — nothing moves until the bar's gauge is full, and right after a move you will not skip straight through a short article or bounce back to the one you just left. Clicking the bar works too. The search box **searches every article at once**: each article in the table of contents shows its number of matches, and articles with none are dimmed. Pressing Next after the last match of an article moves on to the next article that has matches. References by section number (such as "see §9-5") are now **links that jump to the heading in that article**. The split itself did not change the content, apart from a few references that pointed at the wrong section and now go to the right article. On top of that, eight articles — "Navigation", "Tabs", "Drag & Drop", "Right-Click Menu", "Favorites", "Nav Pane" and the newly separated "Address Bar and Breadcrumb" and "Terminal" — have been rewritten with a summary of what you can do, screenshots, and how things work inside, with diagrams. Descriptions that did not match what the app actually does (a "▼" and a "..." button in the breadcrumb, where the ★ is, the tab width settings, the keys for each pane's tab list, the copy/move rule for drags, a claim that overwrite prompts are Windows' own, the order of right-click menu items and when they appear, what the Favorites lock allows and where the export buttons are, the nav pane's outdated "four modes chosen with radio buttons" and its preset names and key, and so on) have been corrected along the way. The terminal material that sat inside the nav pane article is now its own "Terminal" article, and the detailed server material has moved into the FTP/SFTP article.
- **AI Manual answers now list the articles they drew on, and clicking one opens that part of the manual (#345)** — there was no way to tell which part of the manual an answer came from, so reading further meant hunting for it yourself. Up to three source articles are now listed beneath each answer, and clicking one opens **that article at the relevant heading**. The manual excerpts handed to the AI are also re-cut along article headings, with a cap on how long any one excerpt can be (a long section could crowd out the others and keep the part that mattered from being passed along). When the manual is updated, the search index is rebuilt automatically the next time you ask a question.
- **Theme descriptions no longer assume who the theme is for (#349)** — "MintSorbet" was labeled as being for women, "LavenderMist" was described as feminine, and "MilkTeaPearl" as cute, which made them look meant for only some people. All three now describe only the colors and the mood, in all 10 languages. The colors themselves are unchanged.

### Fixed

- **With "Enter inserts a newline" on, Claude Code's confirmation could not be answered** — starting Claude Code in a new folder brings up a "Do you trust this folder?" confirmation, and pressing `Enter` there did nothing. **The arrow keys moved the selection but nothing could be confirmed**, which left almost no way out. The rule that suspends the Enter swap while a choice is on screen only recognised **numbered choices** (the `1. Yes` shape); this confirmation has no numbers, so it went unrecognised and `Enter` was sent as a newline. Choices **without numbers are now recognised too**. Alongside that, the cases that must *not* count as choices — a second line typed inside the input box, or the same symbol appearing by chance in output — are now verified automatically against the real screen shapes.
- **Links in the manual and the changelog did nothing when clicked (#345)** — clicking a link in the text had no effect. The rendering component only announces that a link was clicked, and **nothing in the app was listening to open it**. Web links now open in your default browser, and links to a heading on the same page scroll to that heading. For safety only `http` / `https` links are opened outside the app — a link in the text can never launch a program or a local file.
- **Images inside bullet lists were shown without a width limit or frame (#345)** — screenshots placed inside step-by-step lists spilled out, unlike every other image. The code that tidies images **only looked at top-level paragraphs**. Images inside lists, quotes and tables now get the same width limit and frame.
- **With a display language other than Japanese or English, the AI Manual answered from the Japanese manual (#345)** — the manual exists only in Japanese and English, so asking in French, German and so on always missed on the first search and then fell back to excerpts from the **Japanese manual** (while the document viewer itself showed the English one). For any display language other than Japanese or English, the **English manual** is now searched first. Answers still come back in your display language.
- **Opening the PC view showed "::{PC}" in the tab and the window title (#345)** — when you opened the list of drives (the PC view), the tab, the window title and the nav pane's "Recent folders" and "Browse history" showed the app's internal marker string as-is. Only the breadcrumb and the "opened" notification said "PC", so the name changed depending on where you looked. It now reads "**PC**" everywhere.
- **"Show confirmation dialog when deleting files" was on, yet no confirmation appeared (#346)** — the app never asked by itself; it left the question to Windows. But Windows **does not ask by default** when sending files to the Recycle Bin, so turning the option on changed nothing (and deleting a folder from the nav pane's Tree ignored the option altogether). With the option on, **the app now asks you itself** and deletes only when you choose Yes — from the file list and from the Tree alike. Also, whenever something would be **deleted permanently** instead of going to the Recycle Bin (files on a network, or files too large for the Recycle Bin), Windows now warns you regardless of the option. Before, with the option off, such files could disappear without any warning.
- **The dialog for importing folders from File Explorer showed internal strings instead of its title and instructions (#345)** — with two or more File Explorer windows open, the selection dialog shown by the import button displayed its title and instructions as raw strings such as "dialog.select_explorer.title", because the translations were missing. The text is now in place in all 10 languages.
- **The unit (KB / MB / GB) in the search size filter could not be chosen with the mouse (#347)** — opening the unit list and clicking an item just closed the list without choosing it (only the arrow keys and Enter worked), because a drop-down sat inside another pop-up. The units are now **three buttons side by side**, switched with a single click. The **unit display now follows what you type**, too: before, choosing KB and then typing "500" kept KB on display while filtering by 500 MB (a plain number means MB). Now a plain number shows "MB" as selected, so you can always see which unit the filter will use. The pop-up's hint, which read "tab.size_guide", and the date filter's hint, which read "tab.date_guide", are fixed as well.
- **The drive list opened from "PC" in the breadcrumb showed bare letters like "C:"; it now uses the same names as the PC view (#345)** — the PC view lists drives by name, such as "OS (C:)", but the list opened from the "›" next to "PC" in the breadcrumb showed only "C:", "G:" and so on, making it hard to tell the drives apart. Both now show the Windows display name (the same one File Explorer uses).
- **Folders on phones and cameras appeared in Quick Jump and the history as strings like "usb#vid_…" (#345)** — after you had opened a folder on a phone or similar device, Quick Jump's "Recent folders", the nav pane's Browse history and Recent folders, and the tab title showed an internal Windows string as the name. Folders with a name now show that name, and things without one, such as the device itself or its storage, show "Portable device". Unopenable paths left in the history by a development build (the app's own folder with a device path tacked on) are also no longer offered as candidates and are removed when the history is cleaned up.
- **Opening Browse History could show today's group collapsed, with only the date header visible (#345)** — Browse History groups folders by date and is meant to show **only today's group expanded**. But if you opened it for the first time after the app had finished loading the history, even today's group was collapsed, and its rows stayed hidden until you clicked the header. The "expand today" step only ran **when a group was swapped in while the list was already on screen**, so it never ran when the groups were all there from the start. It now also runs when the list is first shown. Groups you open or close yourself still keep that state.
- **Copying the path in the PC view copied "::{PC}" (#345)** — pressing the copy button in the address bar while viewing the drive list (the PC view) put the app's internal marker on the clipboard, a string that is no use anywhere you paste it. It now copies "**PC**" (typing "PC" in the address bar takes you back to the PC view).
- **Typing a folder that does not exist into the address bar and pressing Enter did nothing (#345)** — when the folder was not there (a typo, for example), you simply stayed put with nothing on screen, so you could not even tell whether Enter had registered. A "**Folder not found**" notification now appears with the path you typed. What you typed stays in the box, so you can fix it and press Enter again. Opening folders from Favorites or the history behaves as before.
- **Search result tabs were titled just "Search:", so you could not tell which search a tab held (#345)** — the title is meant to read like "Search: md", but when the text was translated the place for the search term was lost, leaving "Search:" alone in every language. With several search tabs open there was no telling them apart. The "Result Check" tab for index searches had lost its folder name the same way. Both show their text again, and a check now makes sure translated text keeps a place for the values it is given.
- **Counts and places were missing from notifications and status bar messages (#345)** — as with the search tab title, the place for a value was lost when the text was translated, so "Copied 3 items to the clipboard" read just "Copied to clipboard", for example. 18 messages — the drive you opened, the counts for CSV and Excel exports and the Favorites backup, the sort column and direction, the view mode, the operation that failed in a copy or move, index update progress and more — now show their values in all 10 languages.
- **Dropping a history or Favorites item on the tree, the breadcrumb or File Explorer moved or copied the real folder (#345)** — items from the history, frequent folders, drives and Favorites are there to open places, and are meant to be dropped on Pane A or B (the hint shown while dragging says so). But if you let go over a folder in the tree by mistake, the tree treated the item like a folder dragged from the file list and **moved the actual folder** it pointed to. In the same way, dropping one on the breadcrumb copied or moved the real folder, and dropping a Favorite on the same drive in File Explorer (the desktop, for instance) let File Explorer **move the actual folder**. These items are now treated as bookmarks that point to a place: **dropped on File Explorer or the desktop, they create a shortcut**, and they cannot be dropped on the tree or the breadcrumb (the cursor shows that you cannot drop there). Reordering Favorites and dropping on the file list or tab bar to open a place work as before, and dragging from the file list or moving folders within the tree still move as before.
- **The right-click "OS standard menu ▶" could show items meant for a different kind of file you had just right-clicked (#345)** — the items in "OS standard menu ▶" depend on the extension of the file you select, as in File Explorer. But the items were remembered **by folder only**, so right-clicking a `.png` within 10 minutes of a `.txt` in the same folder showed the `.txt` items. They are now remembered per extension. Also, the "(limit reached)" note on items that have hit their free-version limit appeared in Japanese in every language; it is now translated.
- **Opening the Explorer-compatible menu on files from several folders (in search results, for example) could act on files you had not selected (#345)** — the Windows right-click menu can only be built for items in one folder. The app passed the selection as if everything were in the first item's folder, so the second and later items were treated as **files of the same name** in that folder. When a selection spans several folders, the Explorer-compatible menu is no longer shown; you are told why and get the app's own menu instead.
- **Applying a nav pane layout preset put your favorites back to how they were when the preset was saved, losing any added since (#345)** — layout presets are meant to save and recall how the nav pane's views are arranged. But saving one also kept a full copy of your favorites, and applying it **replaced your current favorites** with that copy, without asking and even while Favorites were locked. Presets now handle only the view layout and leave favorites alone (favorites stored in older presets are ignored). Also fixed: reordering favorites by drag within a second of renaming one could, a moment later, save the old order again (the next clean exit saved the right order, but a crash before then lost the reorder).
- **The left nav pane's width could get stuck, with no way to drag its divider (#345)** — earlier versions had a button to lock the nav pane's width, and that button is gone. But if you had saved with the lock on, it stayed in effect, **leaving no way to unlock it**, and the left nav pane's width could no longer be changed. The saved lock is now ignored, so the width can always be changed.
- **An icon bar trimmed with the nav pane presets "Compact" or "Standard" went back to the full set after a restart (#345)** — the mechanism that adds views introduced in a new version to the icon bar re-added "every view not on either icon bar" **at every start**, so choosing "Compact" (Favorites only) or "Standard" (five views) brought back about 15 views the next time the app started. The app now remembers which views have ever been on the icon bar and adds only views it has never shown.
- **Calling a nav pane view by key (`Ctrl + Shift + 1`–`8`) could move it from the right to the left and leave the right slot empty (#345)** — a view appears in only one place, and the key looked in the left icon bar first, so a view listed on both sides was moved to the left even while it was showing on the right, emptying that slot. The default layout lists views such as Recent Folders on both sides, so `Ctrl + Shift + 6` moved the list from the bottom right to the top left. **A view already showing in a slot now stays there.** Also, the notification shown on switching used the view's internal name (such as `FrequentFolders`); it now uses the name shown on screen.
- **Deleting a Favorites virtual folder could delete the favorites inside it without asking (#345)** — deleting a virtual folder deletes the favorites inside it too, and this cannot be undone. But the confirmation named only the folder, and with "Show delete confirmation" off everything went without a question. A virtual folder with favorites inside now **always** asks, whatever that setting, and says how many favorites are inside. Empty virtual folders and ordinary items follow the setting as before.
- **A favorite whose folder had gone missing could be treated as a file the next time the app started (#345)** — favorites whose target could not be found were saved as files. So on the next start, until the existence check finished, such a folder was treated as a file, and double-clicking it handed it to Windows instead of opening it in a pane (on a slow network share, that window can be long). Missing items are now saved without a kind, and their kind is guessed from the name when they are loaded again.
- **Importing Quick Access into Favorites ignored the lock and could leave an empty folder behind (#345)** — the Favorites lock stops operations that create virtual folders, yet ⚙ → "Import Explorer Quick Access" still created the Import folder and imported while locked. It also created an empty Import folder when there was nothing new to import. While locked it now does nothing, like "Add all tabs in this pane", and with nothing new it creates no folder.
- **Undoing files moved or copied by drag & drop or paste only undid one file at a time (#345)** — after dropping 10 files, `Ctrl + Z` put back just one, so you had to press it 10 times, because an undo step was registered for each file. One copy or move is now **undone with a single `Ctrl + Z`**. The same goes for drops onto the nav pane's tree and for downloads from a server (if a move onto the tree stops partway, what was moved can still be undone).
- **A copy that failed while overwriting could delete what was already at the destination (#345)** — when a copy fails on a read or write, the app cleans up "the partial copy" before retrying with Windows' own copy. But when overwriting, that cleanup deleted **the file or folder that was already at the destination**. If even one file failed while copying a folder over an existing one, the destination folder was deleted with everything that had been in it, bypassing the Recycle Bin. The cleanup no longer deletes anything when overwriting (moves already had the same safeguard). Overwriting copies are also no longer added to `Ctrl + Z`: undoing one sent the new file to the Recycle Bin without bringing back what it had replaced, leaving nothing.
- **Removing or reassigning `Ctrl + T` / `Ctrl + W` in the key settings had no effect in the file list, where they still opened or closed tabs (#345)** — besides the key settings, these two keys were also wired directly into the file panes, which caught them first whenever a pane had focus, so the original actions kept running. The pane wiring is gone and only the key settings decide (with the default keys, nothing changes).
- **In the nav pane's terminal settings, changing "Sync all terminals" was not undone by Cancel (#345)** — every other option is saved with OK and reverted with Cancel, but this one was saved the moment the checkbox changed. As in the Pane A/B terminal settings, it is now saved with OK and reverted by Cancel.
- **Choosing Paste after right-clicking a folder did not put the items inside that folder (#348)** — Paste on the right-click menu pasted into the **folder that was open**, not the folder you right-clicked. If you cut A1–A10 and right-clicked their sibling folder Y to paste, the destination was the very place A1–A10 already were, so nothing moved; only the dimmed cut look went away, which made it seem the folders had been left behind. Right-clicking a single folder now pastes **into that folder**, as Explorer does (right-clicking empty space or a file, and `Ctrl + V`, still paste into the open folder). Also, when the cut items are already in the destination, you now get a notice that they are already in this folder instead of nothing happening, and the items stay cut so you can paste them somewhere else.
- **Folders placed after the index was built showed "0.0B" even though they had contents (#348)** — folder totals come from the index, and a folder with no files found under it was shown as 0 B. That also happened for folders the index did not know yet, so folders created or brought in later looked empty. Folders missing from the index now leave the size blank; 0 B is shown only when the index has the folder and it really is empty.
- **Items moved, copied, deleted or renamed in the app were not reflected in the index until the next scheduled update (#348)** — nothing moved or removed by paste, drag and drop (including drops onto the tree), delete, rename (including batch rename), `Ctrl + Z` undo, the AI folder organizer or macros was reported to the index. The new location did not appear in search and showed no folder size, while entries for the old or deleted location stayed and kept turning up in search. When the operation finishes, entries for places that are gone are now removed along with everything under them and the new places are added along with everything under them, so folder sizes in the list show up right away. Updates run in the background one at a time, so operations are not held up. Places outside the index (unregistered or locked folders) are left alone.
- **Folders placed from outside the app, such as in Explorer, did not appear in the index until the next scheduled update (#348)** — the app watches the folder you have open, but it only passed changes to the index when the update mode was "Auto-detect changes", and **only for files**; creating, renaming or deleting folders was not picked up. Changes seen in the open folder are now reflected for both files and folders under "Update at regular intervals" too (under "Update only when needed", the index is still updated only when you update it yourself).
- **The index's periodic update restarted its countdown every time the app started (#348)** — the periodic update (every 2 hours by default) counted from app startup, and the startup scan only covered folders that had never been indexed. If you usually closed the app before the interval passed, an existing index was never updated. The time of the last update is now recorded and the count continues while the app is closed; if the interval has already passed at startup, the update runs a few minutes later.
- **The menu opened with the Application key `[≡]` stayed up only while the key was held (#332)** — opening the file list's menu with the Application key closed it again the moment you released the key, so nothing could be chosen (`Shift+F10` worked fine). By Windows convention the Application key opens its menu **when the key is released**. Zenith opened the menu on the key press, so on release the framework's own menu handling ran again and closed the menu that had just appeared. **The Application key now opens the menu on release**, the same timing as File Explorer, and you can pick an item with the arrow keys straight away.

## 最新の変更履歴 — [1.16.4] - 2026-09-24 : マニュアルを機能ごとの記事に分けて読みたいところへすぐ届くようにし、テーマカラーを持たない標準テーマ Plain を加え、インデックスへの反映や貼り付け・削除まわりの不具合を直した

### Added

- **白とライトグレーだけの基本のテーマ「Plain」を加え、はじめて起動したときの既定にしました (#349)** — これまでのテーマには、白とライトグレーだけでできた飾りのないものが 1 本もありませんでした。既定の「standard」はベージュで、ほかの明るいテーマもタイトルバーが濃い色だったり背景に色味があったりするため、「変わったものを使っている」と見られたくない場面で選べるものがありませんでした。「Plain」は、ファイル一覧がほぼ白、サイドバーとヘッダーがライトグレー、タイトルバーは段が分かるやや濃いグレーです。**テーマカラーを持たない**のが特徴で、アドレスバーはエクスプローラーのような白い入力欄、選択中のタブやボタンもグレーの濃淡で表します。青は、ファイル一覧の選択と検索欄のフォーカス枠に淡く入るだけです。はじめて起動したときのテーマ選択ではこれが選ばれた状態になり、テーマの一覧でもスタンダードの先頭に並びます。すでに使っている方のテーマは変わりません。

### Changed

- **マニュアルを機能ごとの記事に分け、読みたいところへすぐ届くようにしました (#345)** — これまでのマニュアルは 1 本の長い文書で、開くたびに全体（約 4,000 行）を描画していました。**60 本の記事**に分け、「画面構成」「基本操作」「検索」などのカテゴリにまとめています。目次でカテゴリと記事を選ぶと**その記事だけ**が表示されるので、開くのも切り替えるのも軽くなりました。記事の下には、その記事の見出しが並びます。これまでのように**スクロールだけで読み進める**こともできます。記事の最後まで来ると下に「次の記事」の帯が出て、**そこからもう一押し**ホイールを回すと次の記事へ移ります（先頭でもう一押しすると前の記事の末尾へ戻ります）。スクロールを止めずにそのまま押し込めます。帯のゲージが満ちるまでは移らず、移った直後は画面に収まる短い記事で連続して飛んだり、逆へ回して元の記事へ跳ね返ったりしないようにしています。帯を押しても移れます。検索ボックスは**すべての記事をまとめて検索**し、目次の各記事にヒット数を添えて、ヒットの無い記事を淡く表示します。記事の最後のヒットまで進んで「次へ」を押すと、ヒットのある次の記事へ移ります。本文の中の「§9-5 参照」のような節番号での参照は、**押すとその記事の見出しへ飛ぶリンク**に置き換えました。分けるときには書かれている内容そのものは変えていません（参照先を誤っていた数か所だけ正しい記事へ直しています）。そのうえで「フォルダの移動」「タブ操作」「ドラッグ＆ドロップ」「右クリックメニュー」「お気に入り管理」「ナビペイン」と、新しく独立させた「アドレスバーとパンくず」「ターミナル」の 8 本は、できることの一覧・画面の画像・内部のしくみと図を加えて書き直しました。実際の動きと違っていた記述（パンくずの「▼」や「...」ボタン、★ の位置、タブの幅の設定、ペインごとのタブ一覧のキー、ドラッグしたときのコピーと移動の決まり、上書きの確認が Windows の画面だという記述、右クリックメニューの項目の並びや出る条件、お気に入りのロック中にできることと書き出しのボタンの場所、ナビペインの「左側のラジオボタンで 4 つのモード」という古い説明やプリセットの名前とキーなど）もあわせて直しています。ナビペインの記事に混ざっていたターミナルの説明は「ターミナル」の記事として独立させ、接続先の詳しい説明は FTP/SFTP の記事へまとめました。
- **AI マニュアルの回答の下に「参考にした記事」を出し、押すとマニュアルのその箇所が開くようにしました (#345)** — 回答がマニュアルのどこに基づいているのかが分からず、続きを読みたくても自分で探す必要がありました。回答の根拠にした記事（最大 3 件）を回答の下に並べ、押すと**その記事の該当する見出し**を開きます。あわせて、AI に渡すマニュアルの抜粋を記事の見出しごとに区切り直し、1 つの抜粋が長くなりすぎないよう上限を設けました（長い節が他の抜粋を押し出して、答えに必要な箇所が渡らないことがありました）。マニュアルを更新したときは、次に質問した時点で検索用の索引が自動で作り直されます。

- **フォルダを右クリックして「貼り付け」を選んでも、そのフォルダの中へ入らなかった問題を修正しました (#348)** — 右クリックの「貼り付け」は、右クリックしたフォルダではなく**いま開いているフォルダ**へ貼り付けていました。同じフォルダにある A1〜A10 を切り取り、隣のフォルダ Y を右クリックして貼り付けると、宛先が A1〜A10 のいる場所そのものになるため何も移動せず、切り取りの半透明表示だけが消えて「移動されずに残った」ように見えていました。フォルダを 1 つ右クリックしたときは、エクスプローラーと同じく**そのフォルダの中へ**貼り付けるようにしています（空白やファイルを右クリックしたとき・`Ctrl + V` は、これまでどおり開いているフォルダへ貼り付けます）。あわせて、切り取った項目がすでに貼り付け先にあるときは、黙って何もしないのでなく「すでにこのフォルダにあります」と知らせ、切り取った状態を残して別の場所へ貼り直せるようにしました。
- **インデックスを作ったあとに置いたフォルダが、中身があるのに「0.0B」と表示されていた問題を修正しました (#348)** — フォルダの合計サイズはインデックスから引いていて、配下のファイルが 1 件も見つからないと 0 B と表示していました。インデックスがまだ知らないフォルダでも同じだったため、あとから作った・運んできたフォルダが空に見えていました。インデックスに載っていないフォルダはサイズを空欄にし、載っていて本当に空のときだけ 0 B と出すようにしています。
- **アプリで移動・コピー・削除・名前の変更をしたものが、次の定期更新までインデックスに反映されなかった問題を修正しました (#348)** — 貼り付け・ドラッグ＆ドロップ（ツリーへのドロップを含む）・削除・名前の変更（一括リネームを含む）・`Ctrl + Z` の取り消し・AI によるフォルダ整理・マクロで動かしたり消したりしたものを、インデックスに知らせていませんでした。運んだ先は検索に出ずフォルダのサイズも出ないまま、移動元や消したものの記録は残って、無くなった場所が検索に出続けていました。操作が終わったらその場で、無くなった場所の記録を配下ごと消し、新しい場所を配下ごと足すようにしています。一覧のフォルダのサイズもそのまま出ます。反映は裏で 1 件ずつ順に行うので、操作は待たされません。インデックスの対象外の場所（登録していないフォルダ・ロック中のフォルダ）には触れません。
- **エクスプローラーなどアプリの外で置いたフォルダが、次の定期更新までインデックスに載らなかった問題を修正しました (#348)** — 開いているフォルダの変更は監視していますが、インデックスへ反映していたのは更新方法が「変更を自動で取り込む」のときの**ファイルだけ**で、フォルダの作成・名前の変更・削除は拾っていませんでした。「一定間隔でまとめて更新する」のときも、開いているフォルダで見えた変更はファイル・フォルダとも反映するようにしています（「必要なときだけ更新する」のときは、これまでどおり自分で更新したときだけ更新します）。
- **インデックスの定期更新が、起動するたびに間隔を数え直していた問題を修正しました (#348)** — 定期更新（既定 2 時間ごと）はアプリを起動してから数えていて、起動時の走査もまだ作っていないフォルダしか見ていませんでした。間隔より短く閉じる使い方を続けると、作成済みのインデックスがいつまでも更新されませんでした。前回の更新の時刻を残し、アプリを閉じている間も数え続けるようにしています。起動した時点で間隔を過ぎていれば、起動の数分後に更新します。
- **アプリケーションキー `[≡]` で開いたメニューが、キーを押している間しか出ていなかった問題を修正しました (#332)** — アプリケーションキーでファイル一覧のメニューを開いても、キーを離すと同時に閉じてしまい、項目を選べませんでした（`Shift+F10` は正常でした）。Windows の決まりでは、アプリケーションキーのメニューは**キーを離したときに**開きます。Zenith は押した時点で開いていたため、離したときに Windows 側のメニュー処理が改めて走り、開いたばかりのメニューが閉じていました。**アプリケーションキーは離したときに開く**ようにしたので、エクスプローラーと同じタイミングで開き、そのまま矢印キーで項目を選べます。
